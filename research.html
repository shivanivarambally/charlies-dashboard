3. Capture is a transferability assumption

The expansion model applies a pooled historical capture rate to a new ZIP. The scatter makes the variance behind that shortcut explicit: established-store capture ranges from roughly 16% to 26%.

4. Inventory has a discrete break, not a slow drift

Chain-wide deliveries followed sales closely for seven weeks, then fell below sales in the week of Oct 19. The item table attributes the risk to three store–SKU pairs that received a catch-up delivery and then no replenishment.

Weekly delivery balance

All stores · units delivered minus units sold

Signal. The final week is units below consumption. That is a replenishment discontinuity, not normal variance at this aggregation level.

Known stockout paths

Item-level evidence at 26 Oct · all three had zero deliveries in the final two weeks

Operational priority. Restore ordering or supplier communication first; the measured complaints concentrate in the affected stores.

5. Candidate ranking is sensitive to one unverified exposure

Six-month net after rent is a screening metric, not profit. Adjust the two dominant inputs to see when the top recommendation changes. SITE-006’s construction penalty is supplied context, not an observed causal estimate.

Model boundary

  • Forecast market spend × assumed capture rate estimates revenue.
  • Only rent and known building charges are deducted.
  • No ramp, fit-out, labor, margin, competition, or cannibalization term.
  • Unknown lease charges are treated as zero, biasing some candidates upward.
  • Construction loss applies only to SITE-006.
Decision use: shortlist + diligence, not an investment approval.
